# Changelog

What changed in each cadaclysm release, for the developers building on the SDK: the
readers, the mesher, the blacksmith kernel, the C API and the wrappers. The newest
release is first; each GitHub release page opens with its own section.

The format follows [Keep a Changelog](https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.1.0/) and the
versions follow [Semantic Versioning](https://semver.org/spec/v2.0.0.html) (0.x: a
minor release may change the API).

## [Unreleased]

## [0.16.1] - 2026-11-25

### Added

- Pictures for a headset from a Web Worker: the Rust SDK's `View::picture_of(&Shot)` and `View::pictures_of(&[Shot])`, and the web View's `picture(options)`, draw one or more fixed cameras side by side into one transferable `ImageBitmap` with each shot's rect in it. On WebGPU the shots are drawn into a canvas of the View's own and handed over with no readback (two 2198 x 2304 eyes in about 15 ms on a desktop GPU, against about 240 ms through `image_of`); on WebGL2 they are read back as `image_of`'s. The shots share one background and are anti-aliased under `Style::anti_alias`. `Worker::post_with_transfer` and `PageWorker::post_with_transfer` post a message with a transfer list, so the bitmap reaches the page without a copy. No C ABI change.
- A headset drawn straight into its own textures: the View's `draw_into` (cadaclysm-view) and the web View's `drawXR`, with `View.create({ xr })`, `gpuDevice()` (for an `XRGPUBinding`) and `pauseCanvas`, which keeps the loop stepping but draws nothing on the canvas. On a Quest 2 a scene runs at 90 fps at full resolution.
- `xr.js` (with `xr.d.ts`) in every web folder with the viewer: `XRDirect` runs a WebXR session that draws each eye straight into the headset's textures; `XRPresenter` runs one on the page's thread, asks a worker for each frame's eyes, and blits the answer turned for the head's rotation since it was asked for.

### Changed

- Texture mipmaps are made by halving 2x2 blocks: a large texture's mip chain on a Quest 2 went from 7.3 s to about 1 s.
- The solid mesh pipelines no longer discard fragments, so mobile GPUs keep their early depth test.

### Fixed

- Booleans and intersections between bilinear patches find the edge two faces share again when it lies on a face's clamped rim; 0.16.0 found nothing along it and reported a piece near the trim (a varying chamfer, for one).
- Drawing pictures no longer makes a new depth buffer and background gradient on every call: a headset session on the web leaked about 1 GB a second.
- The web build no longer calls the deprecated `Object.hasOwnProperty` binding.

## [0.16.0] - 2026-11-24

### Added

- One camera's picture drawn offscreen (`cadaclysm_view.h` 1.34: `cadaclysm_view_image_of`) and the Rust SDK's `View::image_of(&Shot)`: a fixed camera (view and projection matrices, eye), a size of its own, a user-layer mask and an optional background, drawn as one viewport of that size would draw it with no grid, no snap marker, no overlays and no viewport labels, and read back as RGBA rows -- the view's own viewports, cameras, size and window untouched. In the browser the Rust call is `async` and goes through the web View's `image`, so a page built on the Rust SDK can print or save the 3D picture there too. The terminal and render view libraries refuse it (they draw only their own camera); the other wrappers bind it in a later parity pass.
- Viewports in the browser through the Rust SDK: `View::set_viewports`, `set_viewport_label` and `set_viewport_border` on a `View::on_canvas` view split the canvas as they split a window, each viewport drawn with its own matrices and layer mask, and a click picks in the viewport under it with that viewport's camera; the viewport-camera entries (`cadaclysm_view_viewport_camera`, `_set_viewport_camera`, `_set_viewport_rects`) answer there too. The web View's new `viewportMatrices(viewport)` gives what a viewport draws with now, before any frame.
- Surface edits, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_solid_untrim`, `_solid_unstitch`, `_solid_ruled_from_edges`, `_solid_change_seam`, `_solid_merge_surfaces`, `_nurbs_surface_merge`, `_nurbs_surface_tween`, with `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_UNTRIM_HOLES`, `_OUTER`, `_ALL` and `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_RULING_TANGENT`, `_NORMAL`, `_DIRECTION`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Solid.untrim(face, which)` removes a face's holes, its outer loop or both, the outer loop replaced by the surface's natural boundary; a side that lies on an old edge keeps it, so the face stays joined there, and a neighbour that shared a removed hole's edge keeps it as a free edge. `Solid.unstitch(faces, together)` breaks a body into sheets: every face alone, or the chosen faces out (each alone, or together as one body) and the rest by connected component. `Solid.ruled_from_edges(edges, ruling, distance, angle, ...)` hangs a ruled sheet off edges -- tangent to the face, along its normal or along a direction, at a length and turned by an angle about the edge -- exact where the far rail is an affine image of the edge, fitted within the tolerance elsewhere; the faces of a tangent chain are sewn, and corners between edges that meet at an angle are left as they fall. `Solid.change_seam(face, parameter, along_u)` moves the seam of a face that goes all the way round (cylinder, cone, sphere, torus, closed NURBS, a whole-turn revolution along its spin) to another isoline, the shape unchanged; a seam through a hole, a revolution along its profile, and extrusion and sum surfaces are refused. `NurbsSurface.merge(other, tolerance)` joins two surfaces across a side they share into one, exactly, and `Solid.merge_surfaces(other)` does it for two one-face sheets whose faces are untrimmed; `NurbsSurface.tween(other, count)` gives `count` surfaces evenly between two. Each result carries its faces' colours and ids from the faces they came from.
- Opening a file can recognize its bodies: `recognize_tolerance` on the open options (the C ABI, the browser reader module, Python and every wrapper: C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++) hands out every B-rep body with its NURBS planes, cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, and line, circle and ellipse edges, exact -- as `Brep.recognize` at that tolerance would. Off by default; meshes are unchanged; a negative, NaN or infinite tolerance fails the open.
- NACA airfoils, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_naca_parse`, `_naca_points`, `_naca_spline`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_profile_naca`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++), and the document's `curve.naca`. `Naca.parse("2412")` (or `Naca.four(m, p, t)`, `Naca.five(cl, p, t, reflex)`) builds a 4- or 5-digit section exactly from the textbook formulas, the thickness laid off normal to the camber line; `points` gives cosine-spaced exact points, `profile` a closed profile (one cubic NURBS within a tolerance, 1e-5 of the chord by default, and a line across an open trailing edge) and `spline` the control points of an open uniform-knot cubic for page editors, with its error. The trailing edge is closed by default.
- Per-vertex colours on the web View's GPU cloth: `showCloth(cloth, {colours})` takes one RGB in 0..1 per sim vertex, in the cloth's vertex order, over `colour` (a garment's pieces in their fabrics' colours), and keeps them as the GPU moves the vertices.
- A garment pattern's own cloth particle distance: `particle_distance` on the document's `garment.pattern` node meshes that piece at its own spacing (a finer cuff that gathers a sleeve), and a seam between two spacings is counted at the finer one so every sample on it is sewn; garments that set none mesh as before. CPU and GPU cloth.
- Posed placements, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_assembly_part_placements`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_assembly_posed_placements`), the web build, Python and the JavaScript wrapper (Node and the web package); the other wrappers follow. `Assembly.part_placements` gives where every solid under an assembly sits in the world, twelve numbers a part (origin and axes) in `Assembly.parts` order; `Assembly.posed_placements` takes `pose`'s arguments and gives the same numbers `pose(...).part_placements()` would, exactly, without building the posed assembly -- the per-frame call for animating a mechanism, gear trains included. In C the caller passes a buffer of at least twelve doubles a part, and a short one is refused with the size it needs.
- Full-round and rule fillets, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_fillet_full_round`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_fillet_rule`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Solid.fillet_full_round(side, centre, other_side)` rounds a rib's or a tube's top: the round touches all three faces, its radius is solved so it takes the centre face's place, and the side faces are cut back to meet it -- a cylinder along a rib whose sides are parallel, drafted or undercut, ending against flat faces square or oblique; a torus on a flat ring between coaxial cylinder or cone walls. A tapered rib, a disc top and face sets of several faces are refused by name. `Solid.fillet_rule(faces, radius, between, rule)` rounds every edge of a set of faces, or every edge between two sets, that the rule keeps -- all, concave only or convex only (in C `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_EDGE_RULE_*`) -- with `fillet` at one radius, reaching what `fillet` reaches.
- `along` on the document's `sheet.split_by_curve` and `sheet.trim_by_curve`: a frame (`xy`, `xz`, `yz`, a sketch or frame node) whose normal the curve's cutter runs along, read when the node evaluates. A cut drawn on a sketch names the sketch and turns with it when the sketch is tilted or moved, where a `direction` written as numbers would go stale. `direction` still works; giving both is refused.
- Mirrors as patterns, in the kernel: `pattern` and `pattern_feature` take a mirror placement as well as rigid ones, and `pattern::mirror(plane)` gives the original and its reflection, so a mirror of bodies -- or of a feature's tool, cut or joined in the same one boolean -- is a pattern of one reflected instance. The C ABI's pattern calls take them too.
- `Sketch::mirror(entities, line)` in the constraint sketch: copies mirrored across a line and held there by `symmetric` constraints, so editing or dimensioning the source moves the mirror; a point on the line is shared and kept on it, so a half profile closes into a symmetric one.
- `Sketch.mirror` and `Pattern.mirror` in every wrapper, wasm and the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_sketch_mirror`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_pattern_mirror`): Python, C#, Go, Java, Node, C++, Swift, Rust (and its web shim), LuaJIT and Godot. `Sketch.mirror(entities, line)` answers the copies (one per entity, in order) and the constraints that hold them; `Pattern.mirror(plane)` the identity and the reflection across `plane`, for `Solid.pattern` and `Solid.pattern_feature`.
- Document nodes `placements.*`, `solid.pattern`, `solid.pattern_feature`, `solid.nth`, `solid.mirror_join`: `placements.circular`, `rectangular`, `along` and `mirror` make a placements value (read back as `{"type":"placements","count":n,"transforms":[...]}`) that several patterns can share; `solid.pattern` keeps the instances as separate bodies (a list) or joins them into one, `solid.pattern_feature` cuts or joins a tool at every placement, `solid.nth` takes one element of a list, and `solid.mirror_join` is `Solid.mirror_join` as a node.
- Mesh to B-rep, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_from_mesh`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++): `Solid.from_mesh` turns triangles -- an STL, an OBJ, a tessellated part -- into a solid the verbs accept, and `Solid.from_mesh_node` does it for a loaded STL or OBJ node. Vertices are welded within a tolerance and every shared triangle edge becomes one B-rep edge; a watertight, orientable mesh comes back as a closed solid (stray triangles reoriented, volume positive, voids kept), anything else as an open shell, and the report says which: faces, edges, vertices, holes, boundary and non-manifold edges, triangles reoriented and dropped. A `mode` picks how far the triangles are grouped: `planes` merges coplanar neighbouring triangles into one planar face, with inner loops for holes and straight runs as single line edges, so a box's mesh is 6 faces and 12 edges; `prismatic`, the default, merges the planes and fits curved regions with cylinders, cones, spheres and tori, bounded by line, circle and ellipse edges, and falls back to `planes` wherever a fit would not close or would hold the mesh's volume worse, the report counting each kind's faces (`planes`, `cylinders`, `cones`, `spheres`, `tori`). A face that wraps round its axis comes back as one face cut open along an exact meridian, as the primitives are built: a cone to its apex, a ball, a torus, a dome over a pole, a round over a circular edge; rounds and corner balls are fitted to rounding, so they meet the faces they are tangent to and a box filleted all round comes back as its 26 faces, every volume to a billionth; where two fitted faces meet in no line, circle or ellipse (a plane cutting a torus), their edge is the section of the two surfaces; in C the mode is a `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_FROM_MESH_MODE_*` code; `faceted` gives one face per triangle, OpenCascade's `BRepBuilderAPI_MakeShapeOnMesh`, which the reference suite now holds it against.
- STL and OBJ files open in the reader library and the web reader: one mesh node per STL, one per `o`/`g` group of an OBJ (`v` and `f` in every index form, negative indices, polygons fanned into triangles; materials and texture coordinates are skipped).
- 3MF and PLY, read and written, in the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++, Unity) -- the first mesh formats the SDK reads into its node tree. `.3mf` opens its mesh objects, build items and their transforms, components (assemblies, including those kept in other parts of the package as Bambu Studio and PrusaSlicer write them), base-material and colour-group colours (per face with `colors=True`) and its unit; `.ply` opens ASCII or binary files of either byte order with their normals and vertex or face colours, polygons split into triangles. Both write from a node's mesh (`mesh_formats` gains `3mf`, `ply` and `ply-ascii`), from a whole scene (`Scene.save(path, "3mf" | "ply" | "ply-ascii")`: a 3MF shares one object per shape among a build item per placement, a PLY bakes every placement into world space) and from kernel solids at a tolerance with their face colours (`mesh_file(solids, "3mf" | "ply" | "ply-ascii")`). Both are cross-checked against independent readers and writers (lib3mf and trimesh for 3MF; plyfile, trimesh and meshio for PLY).
- Gear profiles, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_profile_gear_involute`, `_profile_gear_wheel_cycloidal`, `_profile_gear_pinion_cycloidal`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_gear_centre_distance_involute`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Profile.gear_involute` builds an involute spur gear (pressure angle, profile shift, addendum, dedendum, root fillet and backlash), `Profile.gear_wheel_cycloidal` and `Profile.gear_pinion_cycloidal` a clockmaker's cycloidal wheel and pinion (round or ogive leaf tips): one closed outline each, the flanks NURBS within tolerance of the true involute or epicycloid and every arc exact, that extrudes, cuts and exports like any other. Each returns a `GearProfile` with the pitch, base, tip and root radii, the tooth thickness and an undercut flag; `gear_centre_distance_involute` places an involute pair, shifts included.
- Planar contact settle, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_contact_settle`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `contact_settle` answers an escapement's question: two profiles turn about parallel axes, the driver held at an angle and the follower urged one way from its previous angle -- where does the follower come to rest? It recoils out of a penetration, otherwise advances to first contact or to a limit, and returns a `Settle`: the resting angle, whether it recoiled, and the contact point, normal and touching segments. Conservative advancement on the exact profiles (lines and arcs in closed form, other segments through their NURBS form) never steps over a thin pallet, and the answer never penetrates.
- Lofted flanges and two more hems, in the kernel, the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper. `Solid.sheet_lofted_flange` lofts a sheet between two open polylines -- a square-to-round, a hopper -- press-brake style: planar facets with a bend of the rule's radius on every crease, so `Solid.sheet_info`, `Solid.flat_pattern` and `Solid.fold_flat` read it like any other part (sample an arc into as many points as facets wanted). `Solid.sheet_hem` adds `rope` (curled round a rod `gap` across until it comes back down to the sheet) and `double` (folded twice and pressed flat, three layers); in C `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_HEM_ROPE` and `CADACLYSM_BLACKSMITH_HEM_DOUBLE`.
- Squishing, in the kernel, the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++): `Solid.squish` lays any faces flat in the xy plane, developable or not, stretching or compressing where it must -- Rhino's `Squisher`. Faces sharing edges flatten together; each flat face reports its distortion (the flat over 3D area per mesh triangle and length per mesh edge, as min, max and mean). The boundary keeps its length by default, and the deformation can be free, mostly or only stretched, or mostly or only compressed (the *only* modes hold in every triangle). Curves and points follow onto the flat, and faces that are all developable are laid exactly as `Solid.unroll` lays them.
- View picking and snapping (`cadaclysm_view.h` 1.30), in every language that binds the view (C, C++, Python, C#, Java, LuaJIT, Node, Go, the web `View`, Rust and Swift):
  - Lines, curves, points and glyphs pick within a pick aperture (`set_pick_aperture`, 4 points by default): on top first, then labels, then points and glyphs, then lines, then the face under the cursor. The pick answer grows `segment` (a line's segment, a curve's Bezier; for a kernel solid -- `show_solid`, the web `showSolid`, a read model -- its kernel edge index, the place of its polyline in `cadaclysm_blacksmith_edge_polylines`, the same in either surfaces mode; for a host's own edge list with no edge order, the View's own edge number) and `distance_px`. A pick the view could not draw answers item 0 and no segment; a lost device or an unread window answers nothing. The terminal library's picks reach no wires.
  - `pick_rect` answers the faces, nodes, segments, edges and points in a screen rectangle (kinds `FACE`, `NODE`, `SEGMENT`, `EDGE`, `POINT` = 1..5), crossing or window; hidden things only under x-ray or on top. The rectangle is clipped to the viewport, and one wholly off it answers 0; a NULL `out` with a nonzero capacity and an unknown viewport are refused, and a lost device answers 0 (on the web, `[]`). The render view library refuses it.
  - Four snap kinds: `CADACLYSM_VIEW_SNAP_END` (segment and Bezier ends), `INTERSECTION` (exact on curves; an edge never intersects itself), `POINT` (`set_snap_points`, with a label; they follow item visibility and layer masks only, and points pick only while the curves layer is on) and `ALIGNMENT` (dashed guides through the last eight snaps, and their crossings); `CADACLYSM_VIEW_SNAP_ALL` is now 4095. The new kinds other than `POINT` are opt-in: a view's default kinds are `CADACLYSM_VIEW_SNAP_DEFAULT` (1279: the 1.22 eight and `POINT`), and a host that passes 255 keeps the 1.22 kinds. The snap's `edge` now answers the number a pick answers as `segment` for the same edge (see Changed). The angle lock (`set_snap_base`) turns snaps that land on no point kind onto multiples of a step about a base. The snap answer grows `label`, `locked_angle` and up to two guides, and the marker draws the kind's name. The input event's snap stays the 1.22 layout, and the event carries the new fields at its tail. A lost device or an unread window answers no snap. The terminal and render view libraries refuse the new snap calls.
  - The wrappers: the Rust `Snap` stays `Copy` (its label is a `SnapLabel`, 32 bytes inline with `as_str` and `Display`; guides through `Snap::guides()`), and Go's `Pick` (`Segment`, `HasSegment`) and `Snap` (`Locked`, `LockedAngle`, `GuideCount`, `Guides`) stay comparable with `==`. C++ gains `HitKind`. The library's version is `cadaclysm_view_abi_version()`.
- View clip planes with caps (`cadaclysm_view.h` 1.31: `cadaclysm_view_set_clip_plane`, `cadaclysm_view_clip_plane`, `CadaclysmViewClipPlane`), in every language that binds the view (C, C++, Python, C#, Java, LuaJIT, Node, Go, the web `View`, Rust and Swift): up to six planes (`CADACLYSM_VIEW_CLIP_PLANES`), set and cleared one at a time, each for the whole view, hide what lies on the side their normal points to -- items, edges, curves, lines, points and glyphs, in picks and snaps too; the grid, text, images and overlays stay whole. Several planes keep their intersection (two a slab, six a box). A plane's optional cap fills the cut of every closed body with a colour, lit as a part is, and an optional hatch draws lines over it at an angle, a spacing in model units (0: a fiftieth of the scene) and a width in points; translucent items are clipped, never capped. Python `View.set_clip_plane(slot, point, normal, cap=, hatch=, hatch_angle=, hatch_spacing=, hatch_width=)`, `clear_clip_plane` and `clip_plane`, the same three in each wrapper's spelling, and `setClipPlane`/`clipPlane` on the web. The terminal library and the render view draw no planes and refuse one; clearing a slot is accepted there.
- View clip sets (`cadaclysm_view.h` 1.32: `cadaclysm_view_set_clip_mode`, `_clip_mode`, `_set_viewport_clip_plane`, `_viewport_clip_plane`, `_set_viewport_clip_mode`, `_viewport_clip_mode`, the `CADACLYSM_VIEW_CLIP_INTERSECTION`, `_CUTAWAY` and `_INHERIT` modes, `CADACLYSM_VIEW_CLIP_VIEWPORTS`), in every language that binds the view: a cutaway mode hides only what every plane hides, so two planes cut a quarter out of a part (OpenCascade's chained planes), the caps filling the faces of the notch; a viewport (0..7) can have planes and a mode of its own, drawn, picked and snapped with instead of the view's, so a section shows in one viewport only, and inheriting drops them; and a plane's optional outline draws the cut as a line on the surfaces along it, its width in points. `CadaclysmViewClipPlane` grows to 120 bytes by the outline: a 96-byte (1.31) struct still sets and reads a plane, with no outline. The terminal library and the render view refuse a cutaway and a viewport's plane, and accept intersection and inherit.
- Offsets as results, in the kernel, the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `NurbsCurve.offset(distance, direction)` moves a curve square to its tangent and to `direction`, to its right seen from the direction's tip for a positive distance, as Rhino's `Offset` of a curve in space: exact for a line and for a circular arc square to the direction, otherwise fitted within the tolerance, its `fit_error` the bound reached; a corner (offset a `Profile` for corner styles) and a fold (an arc's radius used up, a turn tighter than the distance) are refused. `NurbsCurve.offset_normal_to_sheet` and `offset_tangent_to_sheet` move a curve lying on a sheet along the sheet's normal, or square to the curve in the sheet's tangent plane, as Rhino's `OffsetNormalToSurface` and `OffsetTangentToSurface`; a curve off the sheet or crossing a crease is refused. `NurbsSurface.offset(distance)` moves an untrimmed surface along its normal over the same `(u, v)`, as Rhino's `Surface.Offset`: exact for a plane, refitted elsewhere, with a new `NurbsSurface.fit_error`. `Solid.offset(distance)` offsets a closed solid either way, or moves an open sheet to its twin, the moved faces meeting on their offsets' intersection (sharp joins only), each face keeping the colour, id and attributes of the face it was moved from and the edges matched by position; `Solid.offset_face(face, distance)` is one face's offset as a sheet, a solid for a face closed on itself. An offset holds its values: no procedural offset curve or surface is kept. The STEP reader now reads `OFFSET_SURFACE` under a rigid placement (a non-rigid one is still declined), and the OCCT `.brep` reader reads offset surfaces over plane, cylinder, sphere, torus and NURBS bases (cone, swept and indirect-frame bases are still declined). C ABI `cadaclysm_blacksmith_nurbs_curve_offset`, `_nurbs_curve_offset_normal_to_sheet`, `_nurbs_curve_offset_tangent_to_sheet`, `_nurbs_surface_offset`, `_solid_offset`, `_solid_offset_face`. Held against OpenCascade's `Geom_OffsetCurve`, `Geom_OffsetSurface` and `BRepOffsetAPI_MakeOffsetShape`, and against closed forms, in the reference suite.
- Sketch constraints, part 2, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_sketch_add_polygon`, `_chamfer`, `_chamfer_angle`, `_pattern_rectangular`, `_pattern_circular`, `_auto_constrain`, and the constraint kinds `SMOOTH`, `TRANSLATED` and `ROTATED` through `sketch_constrain`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++), and the sketch document kind. `Sketch.smooth` joins a free curve to a line, an arc or another free curve with matching tangent and curvature (G2). `Sketch.add_polygon` adds a regular polygon kept regular: equal sides, a construction circumcircle and incircle, four degrees of freedom left to dimension. `Sketch.chamfer` and `chamfer_angle` cut the corner of two lines by two distances or a distance and an angle, trimming them and keeping the sharp as a construction point with the two driving dimensions. `Sketch.pattern_rectangular` and `pattern_circular` copy any entities, each copied point held by the new `translated` or `rotated` constraint and the step carried by driving dimensions, so `set_value` respaces the whole pattern. `Sketch.auto_constrain` adds the constraints the geometry already meets, keeping each only where it removes a degree of freedom, and with `dimensions` the driving dimensions that leave nothing free.
- Chord-length and asymmetric fillets, in the kernel, the C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_fillet_chord`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_fillet_asymmetric`), the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Solid.fillet_chord(edges, chord, tolerance)` rolls a ball sized so the chord between its rails is the given length -- Rhino's distance-between-rails rail type, Fusion's chord-length fillet -- on every edge `fillet` reaches: straight edges and partial arcs (one radius for all the straight edges of a call; edges whose faces meet at different angles are refused, so round them in calls of their own), whole rims each at its own radius, and spline edges between NURBS faces with the radius varying along the crease so the chord holds; a box rounded by chord c is the fillet of c/√2. `Solid.fillet_asymmetric(edges, face, distance, distance_other, tolerance)` cuts each edge back `distance` along `face` (an index every edge must border) and `distance_other` along its other face, the section the exact conic tangent to both faces -- an elliptic arc, the rolling-ball circle when the distances are equal -- on straight edges between planes, convex or concave, ending square or oblique against planes, joined collinearly or meeting two at a convex corner with matching sections (a box's top rim, mitred); circular and spline edges, curved end faces, chains running into a tangent arc and corners whose sections differ are refused by name. Both are held against OpenCascade in the reference suite.
- Face split, in the kernel, the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Solid.split_faces(curves, faces)` splits a solid's faces along curves in place, as OpenCascade's `BRepFeat_SplitShape`: the body stays one closed solid (a sheet keeps its rim) with more faces, nothing cut away, every new edge the curve itself. Each curve lies on a given face, or on every face it lies on when none is given; curves may overshoot their face, cross each other, close as islands and run over several faces. Exact on planes, cylinders, spheres and tori. Both pieces of a split face keep its colour and attributes; the first keeps its id, the others get fresh ids with the face as their origin. `Solid.split_along_silhouette(direction)` splits the faces along the outline seen along a direction (a parallel view), into the part facing the view and the part facing away -- a mould's parting line, Fusion's silhouette split. A curve through a surface pole (a sphere seen across its axis, a cone's apex) is refused; splitting by a surface or plane tool is not done yet. ### Changed

- Mass properties: a coil's walls (a thread's or a tapped hole's flanks and root) are integrated along their edges in the wall's own parameters instead of over sampled rings. The exact cut's volume check no longer re-measures the cutter's walls or the pieces cut from them, so threading the 60 mm example nut takes about half as long (31 s to 16.5 s on a loaded machine) and its mass properties a third (7.4 s to 2.1 s). The values agree with the ring measurement within its stated error.
- Mass properties: a cylinder or cone with a free-form edge (a threaded bore, a tapped hole's crest) is integrated along its edges' own curves instead of over sampled rings. Threading the 60 mm example nut takes 9 s instead of 16 s, and its mass properties 1.2 s instead of 2.7 s. The bore's area comes out at its exact value to 3e-11.
- Booleans: labelling the pieces of an exact cut reads each piece's sample grid once instead of twice, so a piece too narrow for a probe point (a thread cutter's flank slivers) costs half as much. Labelling the 60 mm example nut's cut takes about 2.3 s instead of 4.2 s, with the same labels.
- Mass properties: the faces measured along their edges (coil walls, cylinders and cones with free-form edges) are read once per measurement instead of once per refinement pass, with the same values. The example nut's mass properties take 0.55 s instead of 0.77 s.
- Mass properties: a face measured along its edges no longer builds the rings the measurement only placed its working centre by; its boundary is sampled from its edges to the same tolerance. The example nut's mass properties take 0.55 s instead of 0.9 s, and threading it about 1 s less, with the same values.
- Booleans: cuts with screw threads, tapped holes and coils now take the exact route. Their helical walls are recognised from the geometry and solved in closed form against coaxial planes, cylinders and cones, and the new edges carry exact parameter-space curves.
- Booleans: a body with one free-form face no longer sends the whole cut to the mesh route. Only a pair of faces the exact route cannot solve does, and the fallback reason names that pair.
- Booleans: walls two bodies share over part of their extent, and twin edge chains that run together over part of their length, are met on the exact route instead of the mesh route, so more joins and cuts come out exact.
- Cloth: a new cloth runs the intersection analysis -- it finds where the cloth has passed through itself and pulls it back out -- and edge-to-edge contacts, on the CPU and the GPU, and keeps them through `set_options`. Sewing closes the pairs held open where three pieces meet, and welds a corner's companion pairs with the pair that welds.

### Fixed

- The mesher closes a sliver face thinner than the tolerance whose two long edges cross along it -- a twisted ribbon, as Rhino's tween loft leaves between two spans. Read from 3DM it was refused, and from STEP it lost triangles; either way the solid was left open along the sliver, where it now meshes watertight.
- 3DM: an extrusion with a mitred end is cut by the mitre's plane. It stopped square at the end of its path, so every mitred extrusion read the wrong shape near that end, while the SAT and STEP Rhino writes of the same solid read right.
- A sphere split into two hemispheres along its seam reads as its two halves. Both faces read as the same hemisphere, so the nearest point of the ball to a point just off it could be the great circle, far away.
- `merge_flush` merges the pieces of a read face whose file collapsed an edge shorter than the tolerance into a vertex: their trims meet where the file says they do, though their curves stop that edge's length apart, so the two pieces become one face.
- The mesher no longer asks a knot for a turn on a chord too short for the coordinates to resolve, and judges a short fill over its ring's distinct points: lofts with near-coincident sections that it refused now mesh.

- An offscreen view's `pick` and `snap` answer on a slow renderer too: its draw now waits (at most 10 seconds) for a pick or snap asked before it, so the answer is there after that one draw. On a machine with no GPU, where the view renders in software, a fresh view's first pick could take longer than the wrappers' wait and fail with "the answer never landed".
- Windows: the View's own window opens from any thread. It failed with "OleInitialize failed! Result was: `RPC_E_CHANGED_MODE`" on a thread in COM's multithreaded apartment -- the main thread of a .NET, Java or Go console app, so C#, Java and Go programs needed `[STAThread]` or a thread of their own. The window takes no drops, and now asks Windows for none.

- The closest point of a body, a face, an edge or a curve to a point, in Rust, the C ABI, the web build, Python and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, Godot, C++). `Solid.closest_point(p, max_distance, tolerance)` answers the nearest point of a body's boundary: the distance, the foot, the face, edge or vertex it lies on (with its index and, for an edge, the fillet-table index), the `uv` when it lies inside a face, the normal (the face's outward normal; at an edge or a vertex, the direction from the foot to the point) and `refined`, which says whether it is the exact minimum or the mesh's, within `[d - 2/3 tolerance, d + tolerance]`. Beyond `max_distance` it answers none. `Solid.projector(tolerance)` builds the search once for many points. `Solid.face_closest_point(face, p)` and `Solid.edge_closest_point(edge, p)` answer on one trimmed face or one edge, exactly, and `NurbsCurve.closest_point(p)` gives a curve's nearest parameter and point with a `unique` flag where the answer is a pick among equals. Checked against closed forms and a dense sample of each body.
- Fairing a curve, and curvature checks a test can fail on. `NurbsCurve.fair(points, degree, tolerance, no_inflection)` gives the smoothest curve of degree 3 to 7 within the tolerance of every point, through the first and the last: least squares plus the squared derivative of curvature, weighed as heavily as the tolerance allows, so a design line traced off a drawing comes back with a calm comb where `fit` follows the tracing's wobble. `NurbsCurve.faired` does the same from a curve. `no_inflection` holds the curvature to one side over the whole curve or a span of it, and is refused, with the nearest distance found, when no curve within the tolerance can keep it. The checks: `NurbsCurve.inflections` (where the comb flips side), `NurbsCurve.knot_continuity` (the largest tangent turn and curvature jump at the knots, with where each is) and `Solid.edge_continuity(edge, samples)` (the worst gap, normal angle and curvature difference between the two faces of any edge of a body, of any surface kind, as a `Continuity`). Kernel (`cadaclysm::nurbs::fair`, `inflections`, `curve_continuity`), C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_nurbs_curve_fair`, `_faired`, `_inflections`, `_knot_continuity`, `cadaclysm_blacksmith_edge_continuity`), Python (native and in the browser), the kernel's wasm module and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, C++ and Godot, with a `KnotContinuity` record each).
- Stairs in the BIM editor. A stair follows a walk line of straight segments and arcs at a width, so one rule lays out straight, L and U flights, landings or winders at each corner, curved flights and spirals. It takes the fewest risers that keep each one under its type's largest, from its level to the next one up, so moving a level re-steps it; the going is fixed, so the steps set the last segment's length. Stairs cut the floors they rise through wherever they need headroom. Plans draw them with a break line, the part above dashed, an arrow and UP, and show the part seen through the opening on the level above with DN. Sections cut and fill them, their rails come with them, and the IFC export writes each as a stair with its flights, landing slabs and railings. The Stair tool draws a path or a spiral with a live preview of the treads and the risers left; Properties switch a corner between landing and winders and bend a segment; the sample house has a U stair up from the kitchen. The kinds (`bim.stair`, `bim.stair_type`, `bim.stair_body`) are ordinary document nodes, so the C ABI and Python add and read them.
- Dimensions in the BIM editor. A dimension names references on elements -- a wall's faces, centre and ends, an opening's centre, jambs, sill and head, a column's faces or a round column's outline, a beam's sides, a floor's edges, top and underside, a level's line, a roof's eave and ridge, a stair's walk line -- and follows them as they move: linear chains (horizontal, vertical or aligned, with their total), angles, radii, and height marks in sections and elevations. A lost reference drops its witness and the chain re-labels; one left too short draws nothing and says why. The Dimension tool snaps to the references a plan, section or elevation offers and previews as it goes; Properties edit the references (Add point, Remove), the line drags, the project sets the unit (mm, cm, m or feet and inches), decimals and fraction, and the section SVG export draws them. `bim.dimension` is an ordinary node, so the C ABI and Python add it and read its texts; `cadaclysm_bim_dimension_refs` and `cadaclysm_bim_dimension_preview` give the web build the references and a layout.
- The panel verbs in every wrapper but Godot (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, C++) and in the web build (Python under Pyodide, and Node over the wasm): `split_along_curve` and `panel_gap` on `Solid`, `offset_on_sheet` on `NurbsCurve`, in each language's spelling, with Python's defaults.
- A fillet whose radius follows a law along a chain of spline edges. `Solid.fillet_law(edges, [(share, radius), ...])` rounds a tangent chain of creases between NURBS faces -- a car's shoulder line drawn across several patches -- with the radius given at stations along the chain (each a share of its arc length, 0 to 1) and run through smoothly by a monotone cubic, so it never swings past its stations: `[(0, 4), (1, 12)]` grows from 4 at one end to 12 at the other. Where the faces either side change from one edge to the next across smooth seams, the fillets meet on one shared end. `fillet` with one radius now rounds such a chain too, where it refused the vertex between as a corner. Kernel (`fillet_law`), C ABI (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_fillet_law`), Python (native and in the browser), the kernel's wasm module and every wrapper (C#, Go, Java, Node, Rust, Swift, LuaJIT, C++ and Godot).
- OpenSCAD files read as exact B-reps where the kernel is linked: primitives with the `$fn` rule, transforms, booleans, coloured regions, 2D shapes and extrusions build kernel solids, polyhedra become planar solids and anything else falls back to facets. `Solid.open("part.scad")` hands over the body like any other CAD file, and it writes to STEP.
- Elevations in the BIM editor: Add elevations places North, South, East and West round the building in one step, and an Elevation tool places more. An elevation shows what lies beyond its line with hidden lines removed and nothing cut, the building's outline drawn heavier and a ground line along the lowest level; it opens, selects and moves levels as a section does, and downloads as SVG (ground 0.7 mm, outline 0.5 mm).
- Dimensions in the BIM editor (`bim.dimension`): linear, aligned, angular, radial and height dimensions laid out on plans, sections and elevations, each view drawing only those whose references it draws; an under-counted dimension warns instead of failing its view, and Python reads a dimension's texts.
- Documents hold more than numbers. A parameter is a number, a quantity, an expression, a boolean, text, a list or a record (`{"M4": 4.5, "M5": 5.5}`), nested. Expressions work on all of them: text in quotes, joined with `+` and written from a number by `text(x)`; record literals; `x[i]` (from 0, negative from the end) and `x['k']`; `sum`, `min` and `max` of a list, `column(rows, i)`, `range`, `repeat`, `zip`, `len` and more. Text fields such as a label take an expression too. A `check` node states a condition the document must satisfy -- false fails the node with its message, naming the first failing item of a list -- so a parametric part can refuse sizes that do not fit. Errors on a list node name the item, counting from 0. The site has a new Document format page: every key of the file, parameters, nodes, expressions and their functions, lacing and checks, for reading, writing or generating a document by hand.

A crease whose faces meet tangent at a free end -- a windshield and a roof sharing one smooth centreline -- tapers to a point there. Three methods, as Rhino has: `plain` blends control point by control point (curves of one degree and knot structure; Rhino's own blend, matched to 1e-12), `matching` first makes the curves compatible exactly -- rational curves stay rational, an arc stays a conic -- and `sampling` divides both by arc length, blends the samples and fits a cubic through them (closed curves give a periodic tween). - A face reference finds its face again after a resize: where a resize moved its plane and no face of that kind passes through the remembered point any more, `find_face` (`cadaclysm_blacksmith_find_face` in C and wasm, `Solid.find_face` in Python and the other wrappers) takes the face of that kind facing the same way -- the only one, or the one clearly nearest -- within the face's own size of the point. Before, it found none (-1). A face whose surface still passes through the point is no longer taken by its nearest boundary when that boundary is farther off than the face's own size: that is -1 now. `face_ref` takes a ring face's point on the face itself, not in its hole, so a washer or a flange's face is found by its own point.
- `join_sheets` sews T-junctions: at a tolerance above zero, a naked edge with another sheet's corner lying on it -- a patch side that is a chain of curves beside a neighbour's single curve -- is first cut there, and its pieces are sewn to the edges along it (a cut exact curve stays exact, a NURBS in NURBS pieces). `JoinReport.naked_before` now counts the naked edges once those cuts are made, so `naked_before - 2 * sewn == naked_after`. In Rust the cut is `TrimmedBrep::split_free_edges_at_ends`.
- The verbs that need a manifold solid refuse a merge's non-manifold body and point to its regions (`BuildError::NonManifoldOperand`: "the join needs a manifold solid, and this one has edges of three or more faces (a merge): take its regions with region_solid"): `join`, `cut`, `common`, the splits and trims, push-pull, fillet, chamfer, `shell`, `thicken` and the mass. The STEP writer refuses such a body too ("write its regions"), per part in an assembly. A body is refused when some finite region is bounded by face sides of both senses -- a cell between two others -- so a solid with a cavity and several solids in one body are taken as before.
- The collider's broad phase for large assemblies: from 1,000 parts `Collider.check` finds the candidate pairs with an R-tree over the parts' world boxes instead of testing every pair, 3.5 times faster at 1,000 parts and 15 times at 5,000. The hits, the stats and the pairs a `share` splits are the same as before.
- An edge is closed, for the arc-length divides, when its range is its curve's whole loop, no longer when its ends lie within the length error of each other, so the answer no longer moves with the accuracy asked. An ellipse arc crossing angle 0 can now be measured and sampled. Refcheck compares closed edges from a canonical start instead of setting them aside.
- `health` and `healed` (`Brep.health`, `Brep.healed`, `Solid.health`, `Solid.healed` in every wrapper) are faster, with the same answers to the bit -- 2.6 times at the median over the healing census's bodies that took 5 s or more, up to 28 times where a body's edges stand off its faces: a surface inversion stops once its step stands still and keeps, while a face's rings are built, the searches it has run; a band round a cylinder, cone, sphere or torus is vouched for by a row integral with the surface's own area element before any full quadrature; and a heal that changes nothing reports its input's health instead of computing it again. The healing census's four largest samples (Cruise_Assembly.stp, ufi.stp, Scania-8x4.stp, audi_r8.sat), which ran out of time, now heal every body (`docs/healing-census.md` §6). The mesher and the area and mass integrals share the held inversions.
- Bodies glued along shared edges take the exact boolean route: where two operands' edges are twins (the same edge, as the halves of a split carry it), their faces abut on one surface, or they meet at shared vertices and corner slivers, the meeting is read as that instead of refused, so a sphere split into caps and joined back is now an exact union.
- The Kiln is a cage modeller only: no solid mode, booleans, fillets or shells; Export STEP converts the cage to the exact NURBS solid, and STL, OBJ and glTF write its smooth mesh. The SDK's conversion, booleans, fillets and shells are unchanged.
- Showing items into a View is cheap: a show keeps its item, and the next frame (`draw`, `image`; in the C ABI `cadaclysm_view_draw` and `cadaclysm_view_image`), pick or `renderer_mut` merges and uploads every item shown since in one go, where each show merged and uploaded every earlier item again. 400 small solids now take 0.1 s to show and draw instead of 17 s, and a car's 474 parts no longer spend ~25 s in shows before the first frame. A show is still refused at once where the items with it would not fit the device's buffers; a GPU rejecting the upload itself is now reported by that frame (`take_status`, a false `cadaclysm_view_draw` or `cadaclysm_view_image` with the reason), the items staying shown, and no longer by the show. `View::settle_shows` uploads them now, returning that failure, for a host that wants it at a point of its own; `View::export` draws what the last merge uploaded.

### Removed

- Breaking, Rust: `cadaclysm_blacksmith::BuildError::NoExactScale` and `cadaclysm_brep::NO_EXACT_UV_SCALE`, with the refusal they named (see Changed). A map's refusal is `BuildError::Transform` now, its sentence one of `cadaclysm_brep`'s constants (`SINGULAR_MAP`, `NON_FINITE_MAP`, `NO_EXACT_NURBS`, ...): match the variant, not the text.
### Fixed

- `health` reports the small holes of STEP and SAT bodies, and `healed` fills them. Their holes come in as loops of no stated kind (a STEP `FACE_BOUND`, every SAT loop), and the small-hole test took only a loop marked `Inner`. Now a loop of no stated kind is a hole beside a loop marked outer, or when another loop of its face encloses more of the surface's parameters, whatever order the file wrote them in; a loop that winds round a closed surface (a cylinder's rim) never is. `samples/pinhole-sheet.stp` and `.sat` join the `.brep`, with C ABI and Python tests.
- IGES: a surface that a trimmed or bounded surface stands on is placed by its own transformation. A boundary given only in model space was projected onto the surface as the file wrote it, unplaced, so a surface of revolution with a matrix of its own read as a fold of no area at the origin. Each of the 116 surfaces of revolution in `AssemblyExample-Assembly.iges` carries such a matrix; its first part, a cylinder, now lands in the box the assembly's STEP export gives it.
- Python kernel module: an index or count past 32 bits, negative or not whole (`2**32`, `-1`, `0.5`) is refused with the call's name instead of wrapping silently to another index (on the native and the web backend alike).
- A View filled with many items before it draws -- an offscreen view of a car's 500 parts, shown one by one and saved once -- no longer runs out of memory: every show uploads the whole scene again, and nothing handed the queue the copies those uploads wrote, so each upload's staging and the buffers it replaced were held until the first draw (9.4 GB for the car, then "the GPU rejected the uploaded scene: ... Buffer with 'line occlusion' label is invalid"). Each upload now submits its copies and frees what the device has finished with; the same car peaks at 0.3 GB.

Every drawing of a curved body therefore gains a `<g id="...-silhouettes">` group and refits its page around it; a planar body (a cuboid) gains nothing, having no silhouette curves. Breaking: read `m.Positions()` where you read `m.Positions`. The methods exist so a `Mesh64` held across `Scene.ForgetMeshes()` can answer an error wrapping the new `cadaclysm.ErrStaleView` instead of a slice over freed memory.
- Web viewer: `attachControls` and `forwardControls` now also take keys while the canvas has focus (a key with Ctrl, Meta or Alt stays the browser's; a held key is let go when focus or the page leaves) and pick on a tap, and the wheel zooms in proportion to how far it turns. The Python module needs a 1.4 view library.

- Rust SDK, breaking: the `viewer` module is now `view` and its `Viewer` is `Library`, with no alias (`use cadaclysm_sdk::view::{self, ViewOptions}`; `view::use_terminal()`, `view::current()`). `show()` and `view()` draw solids, scenes and nodes by handle -- exact, in their own paint -- instead of handing triangles over; on the wgpu library `show()` opens a window and returns when it is closed, on the terminal it still prints one picture. `Pose::show`/`Pose::view` draw the scene by handle posed through `cadaclysm_view_set_node_transforms`; a singular rest transform is now the view library's refusal. `Solid::show`/`Solid::view` take `&self` (was `&mut self`). The SDK needs a view library of `cadaclysm_view.h` 1.4 or later.

- Java and C#: `Cad.java`/`Cad.cs` and `Blacksmith.java`/`Blacksmith.cs` now refer to the new `View.java`/`View.cs` (for `show()`/`view()`), so the three files compile together; add `View.java`/`View.cs` to an existing build.   a block, a cut whose wall lands on an existing one -- now decide coincidence at the same tolerance the

## [0.1.0] - 2026-09-15

The first public release: CAD file readers, the B-rep mesher and the blacksmith modelling kernel behind one C ABI, with Python, C#, Go and Java wrappers, for Windows, macOS and Linux.

### Added

- STEP reader (AP203, AP214, AP242) driven by the EXPRESS schema, with the schemas built in: exact B-rep geometry (analytic surfaces, NURBS, surfaces of revolution and extrusion, trimmed curves), open shells, wireframes, colours, and the assembly graph read as a placed, instanced tree. Files in legacy encodings and UTF-16 read with their names intact.
- IFC reader (IFC2X3, IFC4, IFC4X3): products with unit scaling, faceted and advanced B-reps, extrusions and swept solids as exact surfaces, CSG and half-space clipping, openings cut from walls, parametric steel sections, the spatial hierarchy, colours and transparency, and shared shapes read once and instanced.
- SAT reader: bodies and lumps with exact trimmed faces, analytic and spline surfaces, tolerant edges, wire bodies as free curves, named attributes as node properties, and per-face names and colours.
- 3DM reader: B-reps, extrusions, SubD objects, curves, text, block instances, layers, visibility and object colours.
- IGES reader (trimmed and bounded surfaces, surfaces of revolution, space curves, colours) and OCCT .brep reader.
- OpenSCAD: the full language (functions, modules, include/use), 2D shapes and extrusions, CSG, `hull()`, `offset()`, `text()` and per-object colours; zip archives open with a member picker.
- One B-rep mesher for every format: constrained Delaunay triangulation of trimmed faces, crack-free shared edges, a quality setting, triangle caps per face and body, progress, and a report of faces that could not be meshed.
- Edges, curves and surface isocurves for every format, as polylines or as exact rational Bezier segments; faces can also be handed over as trimmed Bezier patches for drawing on the GPU without meshing.
- Levels of detail, meshlets with a crack-free simplification hierarchy, oriented boxes, primitive fitting, convex hulls, collision bodies and signed distance fields.
- A query language (`cadaclysm_query`) that selects nodes by name, property and containment.
- The C ABI: open from file or memory with one options struct, an assembly tree with typed attributes, lazy per-part meshing, placements and instancing, diagnostics, cancellation, format lists for file dialogs, mesh export, and axis conventions (Y-up, Z-up, custom).
- The blacksmith modelling kernel (`cadaclysm_blacksmith`): exact primitives, sketches with lines, arcs and splines, extrude, revolve, sweep and loft, drafted extrusions, exact booleans (join, cut, common), fillets, chamfers and shells, face and edge selectors, sheet operations, meshing and STEP export (AP203).
- Wrappers: Python (reader and kernel), C#, Go and Java (JDK 22+, FFM API).
- Licensing: license file lookup and offline verification, with license info and build date calls in the C API and the wrappers.
